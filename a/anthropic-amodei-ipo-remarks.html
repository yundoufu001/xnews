阿莫代谈 Anthropic 上市：把一百年医学进步压缩进十年，安全执念写进公司基因

Anthropic 首席执行官 Dario Amodei 在上市当天发表讲话：从父亲的病谈起，讲 AI 如何把一百年医学进步压缩进十年，以及为什么安全执念要写进公司基因。

Anthropic（@AnthropicAI）· 原帖 2026-11-19T17:01:26+00:00 · 收录 2026-11-20T08:35:00+08:00

Anthropic 首席执行官 Dario Amodei 在公司上市当天发表讲话，讲了 Anthropic 为什么成立，以及上市之后安全工作要怎么走。

从父亲的病，到把一百年压缩进十年

Amodei 说，他和联合创始人创办 Anthropic，是因为相信这个时代最强大的技术应当为全人类谋利。他和 Daniela 很早就失去了父亲，父亲所患的疾病在几年后就有了治愈方法。他后来投身生物学，希望未来的疗法能来得更快。在实验室里，他看到科学家理解疾病、开发疗法时面对的复杂程度。

他提到，基因编辑、基因合成、用光控制脑细胞这类工具，最初都只是纯粹的想法，也就是思考的产物，也就是智能的产物。人类智能已经让鼠疫、天花、脊髓灰质炎在世界大部分地区成为历史，但其他疾病的进展对太多患者和家庭来说还是太慢。从一个好想法到一种疗法可能要花几十年：从发现 DNA 里一段奇怪的重复模式，到批准首个用 CRISPR 治疗疾病的疗法，中间隔了很多年。

大自然里到处都是这样的模式，但人类一直没有足够的智能去解读其中大部分，也没有足够多的科学家和足够多的时间。现在，这种智能可以造出来了。在蛋白质折叠、药物设计、癌症遗传学、临床试验管理等领域，科学家发现 Claude 能帮他们用几年前所需时间的一小部分完成研究。Amodei 相信，足够强大的 AI 与人类一起工作，有可能把一百年科学和医学进步压缩进十年。

他也给这句话留了余地：目前看到的还只是早期结果，实验和临床试验仍然需要时间。这些过程缓慢而笨重，但 AI 可以大幅加快它们。他希望，未来更少的家庭会因为只差几年而错过一种疗法。

医学只是一个例子，风险也真实存在

医学只是其中一个生动的例子。Amodei 说，Anthropic 的客户包括全球最有雄心的公司、非营利组织和个人，他们已经在用 Anthropic 搭建的智能刺激经济增长、帮助人们摆脱贫困、保护民主和自由社会，甚至让车管所这类日常服务运转得更好。他对未来能一起做成的事非常乐观。

与此同时，他承认这项技术存在非常真实的风险：个人或国家滥用、经济动荡、失去对 AI 系统的控制。他谈论这些风险，是因为它们可以避免，并非认定它们不可避免。

能不能避免，取决于搭建 AI 的人怎么做选择。很少关注安全和执着于安全，两种做法带来的结果天差地别。对安全和公共利益的执着从一开始就写进了 Anthropic 的基因：防范生物和网络风险的保障措施、对齐训练与测试、像给模型拍 X 光片一样看清模型内部，Anthropic 在理解和引导 AI 系统行为的方法上走在前面。

最难的安全工作还在前面

他还表示，还有很多工作要做，最难的安全工作还在前面。造出最有能力的 AI 系统的那批人才，今后要把更多精力放在反复核查这些系统是否安全可靠上。

他明确表态，Anthropic 会把握训练和部署的节奏，花必要的时间确认系统安全、确认系统始终处于人类控制之下，不会向商业或政治压力低头。上市让公司的财务状况更强，这只会增强他们做正确事情的决心。

他最后提出「向上竞争」：如果 Anthropic 能证明在安全上做出正确选择的同时仍能建成一家非常成功的公司，同行就会受到启发跟着做。向上竞争运转正常时，哪家公司的模型最好并不重要，因为大家都在安全地搭建，所有人都是赢家。这种竞争在有人领跑时效果最好，Anthropic 希望并期待自己来领跑，但目标是让整个行业变好，不管 Anthropic 在任何时候是领先还是落后。

他收尾说，机器智能的出现是人类历史上关键的时刻之一，塑造它的机会难得。这件事比任何一家公司、任何一个国家都大，需要所有人一起努力，才能让这项技术向好的方向发展。从今天起，他希望读者加入这项使命。

Remarks from Anthropic CEO @DarioAmodei on our initial public offering:

My co-founders and I started Anthropic because we believe that the most powerful technology of our time should be built for the benefit of humankind.

Daniela and I lost our father to a disease that, just a few years later, became curable. I started my career in biology, hoping to help future cures come sooner. In the lab, I saw the vast thicket of complexity that scientists face when they’re trying to understand disease and develop new treatments.

So many of the scientific tools that can cut through that thicket—tools for gene editing, gene synthesis, controlling brain cells with light—started as pure ideas. In other words, they came from thinking. From intelligence.

Human intelligence has already made diseases like plague, smallpox, and polio history for most of the world. But progress on other diseases has been too slow for so many patients and their families. Going from a great idea to a treatment can take decades: it took many years from the first discovery of a strange repeating pattern in DNA to the approval of the first treatment for a disease using CRISPR.

Nature is full of these patterns. But we’ve had too little intelligence to decipher most of them; too few scientists, with too little time. Now we can build that intelligence. Whether it’s protein folding, drug design, cancer genetics, or clinical trial administration, scientists across many areas of biology and medicine are finding Claude can help them do their research in a fraction of the time it would’ve taken just a couple of years ago. I believe sufficiently powerful AI, working in concert with humans, could compress a century of scientific and medical progress into a decade.

I don’t want to get ahead of myself. At this point all we have are early results. Experiments and clinical trials will still take time. And although they involve slow and ponderous processes, I believe those processes can be substantially accelerated by AI. We hope that in the future fewer families miss a cure by just a few years.

Medicine is just a particularly vivid example, but there are many others. Our customers—the world’s most ambitious companies, nonprofits, and individual people—are already applying the intelligence we’ve built to stimulate economic growth; lift people out of poverty; protect democracy and free societies; even make everyday services like the DMV work better. We’re proud to work alongside them. And I’m extremely optimistic about what we can do together in the future.

At the same time, we have always been honest that this technology has very real risks. These range from misuse of the technology by individuals or nation-states, to economic disruption, to losing control of AI systems. We discuss these risks not because we think they are inevitable, but because we think they are avoidable.

Avoiding them depends on the choices made by the people building AI. There is a gigantic difference between how AI will go if we pay little attention to safety, versus how it will go if we are obsessed with safety. And an obsession with safety and with public benefit has been woven into Anthropic’s DNA from the start. From safeguards against biological and cyber risks, to alignment training and testing, to looking inside our models like an X-ray, Anthropic has pioneered methods for understanding and steering the behavior of AI systems.

There is much work still to be done, and the most difficult safety work lies ahead of us. The incredible talent of the company that has enabled us to build the most capable AI systems will need to be directed, even more so than it has already, towards double- and triple-checking that those AI systems are safe and secure.

And to state it unequivocally, we will pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control. We will not bend to either business or political pressure. Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing.

If we can show it’s possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same. We call this a race to the top. When the race to the top is working properly, it doesn’t matter which company has the best models—everyone wins because everyone is building safely. The race to the top works best when driven from the front of the pack, and we fully hope and expect to do so, but the goal is to make the whole industry better, regardless of whether Anthropic is winning or losing at any given time.

The advent of machine intelligence is one of the seminal moments in human history, and we have a rare opportunity to shape it for the better.

This moment is bigger than any one company, or any one country. It will take all of us, working together, to ensure this technology goes well for us and the many generations to come. Starting today, we hope that you’ll join us on this mission.
