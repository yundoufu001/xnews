JEPA 能在脑子里学会一张世界地图吗：Slow SVReg 让潜空间既保内容又保几何

LeCun 等人的新论文提出 Slow SVReg，解决 JEPA 防塌缩里的内容与几何取舍：1P 中型迷宫成功率 92.9%，城市漫步定位误差 1.6 米。

Turing Post (Ksenia Se)（@TheTuringPost）· 原帖 2026-11-22T00:47:29+00:00 · 收录 2026-11-23T08:25:00+08:00

Turing Post 11 月 22 日发帖，介绍 LeCun、Meng Ye、Ying Wang、Choang333、Kuang Yilun 的一篇新论文。论文问的问题是：JEPA 能不能在脑子里学会一张世界地图。回答是能，但前提是改掉 JEPA 防塌缩的办法。

潜空间规划要同时满足两件事

一个在潜空间里做规划的系统需要两样东西。第一，路径要平滑，距离能告诉你是不是在接近目标，这是几何。第二，要移动的方块还得在地图上，这是内容。常规的 stop-gradient 和 SIGReg 常常只能保住一边，丢掉另一边。

SVReg 先防塌缩，又暴露了取舍

研究者先提出了一个防塌缩的办法，叫 SVReg。它的逻辑是检查表征沿随机方向的 spread，太小才介入，阻止模型给每一帧发同一个编码。去掉 stop-gradient 之后，模型还能改自己编码的东西，丢掉那些没法可靠预测的细节。

但论文标题点出的正是这个取舍。同样的可预测性压力，会抹掉变化慢、难预测的东西，比如一个静止不动、直到智能体去推它才动的方块。

Slow SVReg 加了两样东西

研究者把方案升级成 Slow SVReg，加了两处改动。第一，慢通道：少数固定维度，帧间变化太快要受罚，这个想法借自慢特征分析。第二，门控残差预测器：下一状态预测为当前状态加门乘变化，门初始值 0.1，不变特征预测起来不花代价。

三组数字说明效果

1P 中型迷宫成功率 92.9%，stop-gradient 对照组 21.2%，路径平滑度 1.20 对 5.31，数值越低路径越平滑。 PushT 成功率 87.3%，对照组 44.0%。效率上，只用 20% 的数据，就接近拉满困难 U 形迷宫目标。

城市漫步最有意思

只用 3D 街区的第一人称画面和动作训练，不给位姿数据，Slow SVReg 把智能体位置定到 1.6 米以内，stop-gradient 对照组 3.4 米，未训练编码器 25.9 米。街道布局直接在普通主成分里浮现出来。

防塌缩的选择决定了你拿到地图还是一团点云

JEPA 这条线一直在问同一个问题：什么样的潜表征才够好，能在里面做规划。LeJEPA 让防塌缩可证明，LeHami 把物理当约束加进来，这篇论文说明，防塌缩的选择本身决定了你拿到的是地图还是一团点云。想跟进这条线，先读论文原文，再看 Slow SVReg 的两处改动能不能搬到自己的世界模型里。

Can a JEPA learn a map of the world in its "head"?

This new paper by @ylecun, @mengyer, @yingwww_, @choang333, and @KuangYilun says yes, but only if you change how JEPA avoids collapse.

What's interesting – the researchers firstly proposed a fix, called SVReg, but then discovered its content–geometry tradeoff and upgraded the solution.

Here are the details:

A planner in latent space needs two things:

- Paths have to be smooth, so distance tells you if you're getting closer. That's geometry.
- And the cube you're trying to move has to still be on the map. That's content.

Typical stop-gradient and SIGReg often give you one and lose the other.

SVReg stops the model from giving every frame the same code. It checks the spread along random directions and only steps in when it gets too small. Without stop-gradient, the model can also change what it encodes, dropping details it can’t reliably predict.

But the title itself says that there is "content–geometry tradeoff".
The same predictability pressure erases things that change slowly and are hard to predict, like a block that sits still until the agent pushes it.

So they built Slow SVReg, with 2 additions:

1. Slow channels: A few fixed dimensions get penalized if they change too fast between frames (an idea borrowed from slow feature analysis).
2. Gated residual predictor: Predicts the next state as current + gate × change (with the gate starting at 0.1), so unchanging features cost nothing to predict.

The results:

• 1P Medium maze: 92.9% success (vs. 21.2% stop-gradient) with way smoother paths (1.20 vs. 5.31 jaggedness).
• PushT: 87.3% success (vs. 44.0% stop-gradient).
• Efficiency: Nearly maxes out hard U-maze goals using just 20% of the data.

And the most interesting part is city walks.
Trained only on first-person frames and actions from 3D city blocks (no pose data), Slow SVReg pinpoints the agent within 1.6 meters (beating stop-gradient's 3.4 m and an untrained encoder's 25.9 m). The street layout pops right out in the plain principal components.

So the JEPA line keeps circling the same question – what makes a latent good enough to plan in? LeJEPA made anti-collapse provable, LeHami added physics as a constraint. This paper shows the anti-collapse choice itself decides whether you get a map or a cloud of points.
